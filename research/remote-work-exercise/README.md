# Remote work and exercise: more time, less movement?

> **Parked 2026-11-04.** Sabrina Wulff Pabilonia (BLS) and Victoria Vernon (SUNY
> Empire State) already compare remote, hybrid and on-site workers in ATUS,
> 2017–18 vs. 2023–24, with exercise as an outcome and occupation and industry
> controls (Southern Economic Association, Nov 2025). Their slides: remote men's
> exercise time was greater post-COVID. Their 2022 BLS paper found female home-based
> teleworkers spent 10–11 more minutes on sports and active leisure. A
> walking-to-work variant (exercise plus active commuting) was considered and dropped.
> Sources: https://www.bls.gov/dpr/working-papers/how-did-the-remote-work-revolution-change-our-work-and-leisure-time.pdf
> · https://www.bls.gov/dpr/working-papers/pabilonia-vernon-2022-wp.pdf

**Puzzle:** working from home saves commute time, yet a 2026 Finnish study of 99
hybrid workers found 45 more minutes of sitting on remote days. Does remote work
raise or lower exercise, and for whom?

## Data
- **American Time Use Survey via IPUMS** (https://www.atusdata.org): free with a
  login, no advisor or IRB needed. Minutes per activity for one day, including
  sports and exercise, and where work happened.
- Lets you compare the same kind of worker on home vs. office days, before and
  after 2020.

## Risks
- The BLS working paper "How did the remote work revolution change our work and
  leisure time?" may already cover exercise. Read it first:
  https://www.bls.gov/dpr/working-papers/how-did-the-remote-work-revolution-change-our-work-and-leisure-time.pdf
- Selection: people who work from home differ from those who don't. Results are
  "linked to," not "caused by," unless there's a natural experiment.
