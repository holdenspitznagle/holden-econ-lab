# Memo: who wins and loses in the GLP-1 economy

**Companion to paper 1 (set 2026-11-05).** A strategy memo, not a research paper.
Shows business and strategy thinking; feeds content.

## The question
GLP-1s change what people buy. Which businesses gain (protein supplements, nutrition
bars, lab testing, telehealth) and which lose (snacks, sweets), and what should
brands do?

## What's known (verify each before citing)
- Cornell study on Numerator purchase data (Journal of Marketing Research, per press
  coverage): grocery spending fell about 5.3% within six months of starting; savory
  snacks fell about 10%; gains in yogurt, fresh fruit, nutrition bars and meat
  snacks. Protein powder not broken out. Find the original paper before using numbers.

## Sources to use
Google Trends (protein, GLP-1 food); company earnings calls; new "GLP-1 friendly"
product launches; the Cornell paper; health-care findings from paper 1.

## Honest limits
- Supplement purchase data is mostly paid, so this memo describes shifts; it can't
  prove cause.
- **Prices:** protein prices move with whey costs, competition and promotions.
  Don't claim GLP-1s raised prices without evidence.

## Shape (2–4 pages)
1. What GLP-1s change about spending. 2. Winners and losers. 3. What it means for
brands (recommendations). 4. Limits.
