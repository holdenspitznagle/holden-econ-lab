# Research timeline and payoff plan

*Set 2026-11-04. Holden: sophomore at FIU, transferring for fall 2027.*

## Timeline

| When | GLP-1 paper | Trades paper | Transfer and career |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nov 2026** | Browse UAS Data Explorer; get a faculty signature; register | Start weekly listings log | List target schools and each one's transfer deadline and requirements |
| **Dec–Jan** (winter break) | Check sample size; run analysis; write a 10–15 page draft | Keep logging listings | Draft transfer essays; the paper is the story |
| **Jan** | Send draft to the professor | Internship starts; trades becomes the main focus | Ask that professor for a transfer recommendation (give 4+ weeks) |
| **Feb–Mar** | Revise; post a working-paper version | Ownership map from public data | Submit transfer applications (check each deadline) |
| **Apr** | Final version; present at FIU's spring undergraduate research conference if timing works | Owner interviews; listings analysis | Content and LinkedIn push around the finished paper |
| **Summer 2027** | Submit to an undergraduate economics journal | Write the draft | Update résumé; plan outreach at the new school |
| **Fall 2027** (new school) | Pitch paper 2 (GLP-1s and health care use) | Finish and post | Bring both papers to professors: RA roles, honors thesis |

Reality check: transfer applications will likely go in with the GLP-1 paper as a
strong draft, not published. That's fine: "working paper, under revision with
faculty feedback" is honest and impressive for a sophomore.

## What you get, and how to get the most from each

**1. A transfer application that stands out**
- The paper is proof of initiative most transfer applicants don't have.
- The professor who signs or reads your draft can write your college recommendation,
  which most transfer applications ask for. This may be the biggest single payoff.
- Get the most: make the essay about the question ("why don't people do what's good
  for them?"), not the paper's details. List it as a working paper, with a link.

**2. A head start at the new school**
- Walk in with a finished paper: professors take you seriously in week one.
- Get the most: email two or three faculty in health or behavioral economics before
  you arrive, with the paper attached. Ask about RA roles. Paper 2 can grow into an
  honors thesis.

**3. A writing sample for jobs**
- Fed RA, think-tank and pre-doctoral jobs ask for exactly this. So do research
  internships.
- Get the most: keep a one-page summary (question, data, finding, limit) ready to send.

**4. Material for your brand**
- One paper can feed many posts.
- Get the most: a LinkedIn post with the one striking chart; an Instagram Reel ("I
  studied whether Ozempic users stop exercising"); an X thread tagging the
  researchers you cite. Plan three to five pieces per paper.

**5. A network**
- Get the most: send the finished paper to authors you cite (the ENDO 2026
  team, economists working on GLP-1s) with one line on what you found. Some
  will reply; that's how research connections start.

**6. Career leverage from the trades paper**
- Credibility with HVAC owners, PE firms and operators; ties directly to the
  internship and the goal of growing or buying businesses.
- Get the most: share the ownership map or valuation findings with owners you meet.
  Turn it into a short strategy memo for PE and operator audiences.

**7. A clear specialty**
- "The economics of GLP-1s and health behavior" is memorable. Each new piece makes
  the last one more valuable.

## Rules that protect the payoff
- Finish before polishing. A done, honest paper beats a perfect unfinished one.
- Every number sourced (`CLAUDE.md` §8). One wrong fact undercuts all of it.
- Grades in math and econometrics still matter, especially for transfer.
